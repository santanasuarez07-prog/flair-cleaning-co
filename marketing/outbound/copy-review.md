# Copy review log: how each version got better

Every new email goes through the **pre-send checklist** below. After each
rewrite or test, add a dated entry: what was wrong, what changed, what to watch.

## Pre-send checklist (score each email; send only at 9/10 or better)
1. **First line is about them** (their problem, their building, or why they were picked), not "I'm Santana..."
2. **One real, specific detail** about that business (street, building type, something seen on a drive-by). Never invented.
3. **60 to 90 words** in the body (footer not counted).
4. **Paragraphs, not bullets.** It should read like a person wrote it.
5. **Relevant to who controls it:** tenants get glass; owners and managers get gutters.
6. **One honest proof point** (100+ Granite Bay homes, a real review), with no business-client claims until they're real.
7. **The process makes it easy:** set schedule, a text the day before, photos after.
8. **A small yes/no ask** ("Worth a quick look?"), never a big commitment ("Want one of the spots?").
9. **In a test, only the hook differs.** Everything after the first paragraph is identical across variants.
10. **Clean:** read out loud, no typos, plain text, 0 links, opt-out footer.

## 2026-11-19: v1 → v2 (all 10 Granite Bay drafts)
**Problems found in v1**
- Opened with "I'm Santana Suarez, owner of..." (me-first). Fixed: hook first, intro second.
- The ask was too big: "Want one of the spots?" Fixed: "Worth a quick look? I can stop by and price it in 5 minutes."
- Bullet lists looked like marketing. Fixed: short paragraphs.
- 110 to 130 words. Fixed: about 80 to 95.
- **B1 and B3 differed in structure, length and CTA**, so the test couldn't show what won. Fixed: identical body; only paragraph 1 changes.
- B3 pitched gutters to dental *tenants*, who usually don't control gutters (the landlord does).
  Fixed: tenants get the glass angle (water spots, mud splashback); managers and owners get gutters.
- Subjects were long and salesy. Fixed: short and specific ("Douglas Blvd route, Dr. Martin", "Rain and your front windows, Dr. Smith").

**Still weak in v2 (to fix next)**
- No proof a human looked at *their* building. **Before sending:** add one true line after a
  drive-by (e.g., "Drove by Tuesday; the lower panes by your door have mud splash from the rain").
- No price anchor. Once the storefront route price is set, test "from $X a visit" as a variant.
- Gatekeeper emails (golf club, Quarry Ponds) have a double ask ("worth a walk? if not, who?").
  That's acceptable for unknown contacts, but switch to a single ask once we have a name.

**What to watch in the data**
- B1 vs B3 reply rate (target > 6%). If both get under 2% after 25 sends each, the problem is
  trust or targeting, not the hook: add drive-by personalization and test again.
- Which segment replies at all (dental vs realtor vs HOA manager). The segment may matter more than the copy.
