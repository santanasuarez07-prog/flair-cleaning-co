# Flair cold email system

The rules every cold email follows. Templates and prospects live in
`2026-11-wave2-trust.md` and `prospects-2026-11.csv`.

## Core idea
People hire who they **trust**. A script fails when the prospect doesn't trust
us: typos, sounding small, putting them on a pedestal, or simply having no
personality. So we **test emotional angles, not generic subject lines.**

## Step 1: Pick ONE angle per test batch
| Angle | The story | Sounds like | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. The Neighbor** | Local, friendly, owner-run, we actually care | "I'm Santana, I live and work in Granite Bay..." | small storefronts, cafes, dental offices, Loomis/Auburn main streets |
| **B. The Pro** (status) | We run routes, systems, photo reports; we're the vendor serious businesses use | "We run a weekly route on Douglas Blvd..." | property managers, HOAs, centers, golf club |
| **C. The Best Results** | Cleaner glass, every downspout flushed, before/after proof, guarantee | "If it's not the cleanest your glass has been, you don't pay." | realtors, luxury, anyone who's been burned by a cheap vendor |

Talk to them **peer to peer**: confident, never begging, no "I'd be so
grateful". We're the expert they need, not the other way around.

## Step 2: Pick a framework (all four adapted to exterior cleaning)

Local service has an edge an agency never has: **we can physically look at
their building.** Every framework uses something real we saw.

### F1. "I noticed something" (observation hook)
**Subject:** Noticed something on your building, [First name]
> Hey [First name], hope the week's treating you well.
>
> I drove past [Business] on [street] this morning and noticed [real
> observation: water stains streaking down the front glass / gutters over the
> entrance sagging with leaves / green buildup on the walkway by the door].
>
> It's a quick fix, and I'd rather tell you than have a customer notice first.
> I snapped a photo. Want me to send it over?

*When they reply:* send the photo, explain the fix in 2 lines and give a price.
The reply buys time to look closer.

### F2. "Quick question" (current vendor)
**Subject:** Quick question, [First name]
> Hey [First name], real quick because I know you're busy.
>
> Who handles your windows and gutters right now?
>
> I clean storefronts and buildings around [Granite Bay] and the thing I see
> most is [hard-water spotting that a squeegee won't remove / downspouts never
> getting flushed, just the troughs]. Most vendors skip it. Cool if I share
> what to look for?

*Works best with angle B or C.* Have the "what most vendors skip" list ready
(below).

### F3. A real teammate's tip (honest forward)
Only when it's **real**: a Flair tech or salesperson actually spotted the
business. Forward their actual text or email. **Never fabricate a forward**:
it's deceptive, it violates CAN-SPAM's misleading-header rule, and one
screenshot of it on Nextdoor kills our local reputation.
**Subject:** FWD: [Business] windows
> Hey [First name], one of our techs, [Victor], was working on a home near
> you and flagged [Business]. Forwarding his note below. Figured it was
> worth reaching out.
>
> If you're open to it, I can stop by and show you what he meant. 5 minutes.
>
> ---------- Forwarded message ----------
> From: Victor
> "Boss, the place on Douglas with the big windows, the glass has hard-water
> stains top to bottom. Easy job for us."

### F4. "Video walk-by" (curiosity)
**Subject:** 45-second video of [Business]
> Hey [First name], happy [day].
>
> I recorded a quick video walking past your [storefront / building] pointing
> out 2 things we'd clean up and how. No pitch, just what I saw.
>
> Cool if I send it here?

*Record it on a phone in 45 seconds. Real, specific and friendly. This is the
highest-effort, highest-curiosity option; save it for top prospects (the golf
club, centers, HOA properties).*

### "What most vendors skip" list (ammo for F2 and replies)
- Hard-water spots: need the right method, not just a squeegee
- Frames, tracks and sills: most crews wipe glass only
- **Downspouts:** most gutter crews scoop troughs and never flush the downspouts
- Screens: removed, washed and dried, not just brushed
- Solar: no pressure washer, no harsh soap (it can void the warranty)
- Before/after photos sent to the manager after every visit
- Showing up on the same day every route, with a text the day before

## Step 3: Follow up twice, never "just bumping this"
Max **3 emails total** (the first + 2 follow-ups). Each one adds **new value**:

**Follow-up 1 (day 3): value**
> [First name], one thing worth knowing even if you never hire us:
> [hard water left on glass etches it permanently after a few months / a
> single clogged downspout can dump 100+ gallons at your entrance in a big
> storm]. Happy to check yours for free next time we're on [street].

**Follow-up 2 (day 7): social proof + real urgency**
> Last one from me. We just [cleaned 3 homes in Granite Bay this week / added
> a business to our Thursday Douglas Blvd route]. A recent customer called it
> "courteous, prompt, professional service." I have **2 spots left on that
> route for December**. Want one?

*(Only claim spots or numbers that are true that week.)*

**After 3 with no reply:** stop emailing. If they're a top prospect, switch
channel: walk in, call, or message them on Instagram, Facebook or LinkedIn.

## Step 4: Test manually first
- **Week 1: 50 hand-written emails in Granite Bay**, sent from Gmail (not automated).
  Each one gets a real, specific line about that business.
- Split them: ~17 per angle (A, B, C). Use F1/F2 for most, F4 for the top 5.
- Read each one out loud before sending. One typo = lost trust.
- Log every send in `cold-email-tests.csv` (angle, framework, subject, result).
- Once an angle wins, **then** template it with merge fields and scale to
  Rocklin → Loomis → Folsom → EDH → Auburn.

## North-star metrics
| Metric | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Open rate | > 60% | Gmail doesn't track opens; use a tracker (e.g., Mailtrack/Streak) or skip and judge by replies |
| Reply rate | > 6% | any human reply, even "no thanks" |
| Book rate | > 2% | walkthrough, free test clean or meeting booked |
| Local bonus | | walk-in conversations and free cleans → recurring route |

**Every reply is feedback, including no reply.** Zero replies from 17 sends on
one angle means the angle is wrong, not the subject line.

## Step 5: Keep improving
- **New script every 2 weeks.** Retire the loser and keep the winner as champion.
- Save every good cold email or flyer you receive to `swipes/`.
- Whenever you drive past a business with dirty glass or packed gutters,
  snap a photo and add them to the prospect list on the spot. That's an F1
  email ready to send.
